## §0. Abstract

A recurring obstruction in the Z-Spin measurement line is that positive structure supplies attenuation but not phase. ZS-S28 records this in four places: a quadrant bound on positive kernels, a quantisation of the argument to {0, π} for real-symmetric compressions and for the Kato holonomy, and the summary statement that attenuation is reachable while phase is absent. The same paper records the complementary fact that positive dephasing together with a Hermitian generator realises any multiplier. Read together these are not an exclusion but a division of labour, and this paper takes them that way.

We type the division. Let a boundary transfer operator P be positivity improving on a cone generated by an orthonormal basis, let ψ₀ be its unit-norm Perron–Frobenius ray, let J be a self-adjoint involution, and let U be the unitary that carries the phase. In this factorisation attenuation selection and phase generation are represented by different structures; whether one object derived from an action could do both is not settled here (SAME-OBJECT-OPEN). Because ψ₀ is real, the two axes of the multiplier a = ⟨ψ₀|U|ψ₀⟩ are **two different real symmetric forms evaluated on one ray**, and |a| = 1 exactly when U stabilises that ray. Two aligned cases follow, and they are not exhaustive: if the involution preserves the cone then [P, J] = 0 forces Jψ₀ = ψ₀ and the seam asymmetry of the selected state vanishes; if instead the involution is diagonal in the generating basis then [P, J] ≠ 0 is forced and the ray carries a grading imbalance κ with |κ| < 1, hence a strictly positive seam asymmetry.

**That is the whole of what positivity supplies, and it is less than either predecessor version claimed.** Under the diagonal alignment, strict positivity bounds |κ| away from 1; it does not bound κ away from 0. We exhibit an exact counterexample in which the transfer operator is entrywise positive, the commutator is nonzero, the seam asymmetry is *maximal*, and the phase is nevertheless identically zero at every angle. Positivity therefore does not select a phase-capable state, and we show further that the reachable set of κ is the whole open interval (−1, 1) in every dimension, so under that alignment positivity constrains κ by |κ| < 1 and by nothing else.

**Outside that alignment it supplies even less.** A second exact counterexample keeps the same entrywise-positive transfer operator and the same non-central self-adjoint involution but rotates the generating basis of the cone: the involution is then neither cone-preserving nor diagonal, the commutator vanishes, the ray is a −1 eigenvector, κ = −1, and the seam asymmetry is *dead*. The surviving half is therefore conditional on an alignment between the grading and the cone, and nothing in the coordinate formulation derives that alignment.

**So we change carriers, and the alignment freedom goes — at a stated price.** Take the carrier to be the algebra of operators on the boundary space, with its cone of positive semidefinite elements, a positive trace-preserving map in place of the transfer matrix, and a fixed state in place of the Perron ray. Once that carrier is fixed the cone is canonical, and three things follow. The strict bound |κ| < 1 holds from faithfulness of the fixed state alone, with no alignment hypothesis, so the second counterexample has no analogue there. The imbalance κ = Tr(ρ J) is unchanged when the map and the grading are rotated together, so no basis alignment remains to choose. And most usefully, **if the boundary map commutes with the grading flip then its only fixed state is flip-invariant and κ = 0 exactly** — so phase capability is not a matter of finding a better positive map but requires the action to break the sector-exchange symmetry, by an amount we bound from below in terms of the map's flip defect.

**The price is stated rather than hidden.** Passing from a vector to a state and from a matrix to a map is not a change of coordinates; it is a lift to an operator-state model, and nothing here derives that lift from an action. We declare it as a construction choice, and we make its cost exact: on the diagonal data the two multipliers agree, but no pure state is faithful, so the strict bound above is bought with mixing that the vector model does not carry and whose origin is not established. What the move accomplishes is a change in the *kind* of open question, from a frame nobody chose on physical grounds to a carrier and a map that an action would have to supply.

The aligned cases fire in opposite directions in the two sectors of the corrected master action. On the vacuum circle the reflection preserves the cone, and every scalar boundary term the printed list *writes down* is even under it, so the explicit boundary-term route closes negatively (SCALAR-SCOPE-V13). The closure stops there, and this revision says so: the upstream action does not specify the scalar potential, so an unspecified potential could still select a reflection-breaking vacuum whose effective boundary weight is asymmetric — an indirect route that is untouched and open. In the fermion sector the chirality operator is diagonal in the boundary basis, and the boundary form of the Dirac term supplies, without being asked, a two-dimensional boundary space carrying a non-central self-adjoint involution — the three requirements ZS-M56 places on the environment, met at once by an imported object.

On the imported chiral-bag family the multiplier is a(θ) = cos θ + i κ sin θ, and the contraction deficit factorises exactly:

> **1 − |a|² = (1 − κ²) sin²θ = T² sin²θ,**

where T is the seam asymmetry ZS-M60 demands of the state and sin θ is the angle the boundary condition supplies. Neither factor alone produces the multiplier, and neither does the pair without the further conditions κ ≠ 0 and sin θ ≠ 0, which are load-bearing gates rather than consequences.

The residue is then one angle *within an externally selected subfamily*. The full moduli space of self-adjoint boundary conditions is U(2), and the chiral-bag U(1) inside it is chosen rather than derived; the missing arrow is registered as a gate. Within that subfamily, a classical chiral reparametrisation of the free-Dirac boundary model shifts the boundary angle by −2β and the Yukawa mass phase by +2β, so Δ = θ + θ_m is the invariant of that reparametrisation, and ZS-S14 v2.1 fixes neither summand. Whether Δ is the physical datum of the full theory is a separate question, because the quantum chiral rotation is anomalous and moves the vacuum angle too; that question is gate F-M66.5.

Finally we adjudicate the sharpest objection. Cheshire-Cat compensation is an anomaly-inflow statement about a pair (interior, exterior); it needs an exterior field to absorb what leaves. We compute both boundary fluxes exactly on the boundary subspace at seven angles: the vector flux vanishes identically — that is the self-adjointness condition — while the axial flux does not, restricting to diag(−1, +1) in the graph basis for every angle including the MIT one. ## §2. Setting

Let 𝓗 be a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space with a distinguished orthonormal basis generating a cone K, and let C be the antiunitary conjugation fixing that basis.

- **P** is a **real-linear** operator on the real form of 𝓗 fixed by C, that is P ∈ End_ℝ(𝓗_C), and is *positivity improving*: P(K∖{0}) ⊂ int K. If P is presented as a complex-linear operator, we require PC = CP so that it restricts to that real form; without such a condition the Perron–Frobenius argument does not apply and no statement of §3 is asserted. "Top eigenvalue" means the spectral radius, which for a positivity-improving operator is an eigenvalue with a strictly positive eigenvector. P is not assumed self-adjoint anywhere in §3.1–§3.4; the symmetric instances of §3.6 and of the companion are instances, not hypotheses. By Perron–Frobenius its top eigenvalue is simple with eigenvector ψ₀ ∈ int K; in particular Cψ₀ = ψ₀. **Normalise ‖ψ₀‖ = 1 throughout.** Write σ₀ = |ψ₀⟩⟨ψ₀|.
- **J** is a self-adjoint involution, J ≠ ±I; κ := ⟨ψ₀|J|ψ₀⟩ and Δ := σ₀ − Jσ₀J.
- **U** is unitary; a := ⟨ψ₀|U|ψ₀⟩.

The normalisation is not decorative. The expressions σ₀ = |ψ₀⟩⟨ψ₀|, κ = ⟨ψ₀|J|ψ₀⟩ and Tr(Δ²) = 2(1 − κ²) are the unit-norm forms; the companion computes the scale-invariant quotients instead. The two agree, and row A09 checks that they agree, but the v1.0 predecessor stated one and computed the other, which is a defect of statement even when the arithmetic is right.

The two objects P and U are kept distinct throughout. That separation is the content of the paper, not a convenience.

### 2.1 The cone is a choice, and it is load-bearing

**The distinguished basis is data, not a normalisation.** Everything in §3 is stated relative to a cone, and every conclusion depends on how J sits relative to *that* cone rather than on the pair (P, J) alone. Row A10 makes the dependence explicit: one involution and one transfer operator, read against two different orthonormal generating bases, land in two different cases of §3.2 — and, as §3.2.1 shows, one of those readings lands outside both.

When §5 applies §3 to the two-dimensional fermion boundary space, it takes the chirality eigenbasis to be the generating basis of the cone. **Nothing derives that identification.** The corrected action supplies no transfer operator on the boundary space, hence no positive structure and hence no cone; the identification is imposed so that the boundary grading is diagonal, which is precisely the hypothesis branch (b) needs. ## §3. The split

### 3.1 Two forms on one ray

Because ψ₀ is real, a\* = ⟨ψ₀|CUC|ψ₀⟩, hence with U_C^± := (U ± CUC)/2,

> **Re a = ⟨ψ₀|U_C⁺|ψ₀⟩,  Im a = (1/2i)⟨ψ₀|(U − CUC)|ψ₀⟩.**

In the cone basis this reads Re a = ψ₀ᵀ·Sym(Re U)·ψ₀ and Im a = ψ₀ᵀ·Sym(Im U)·ψ₀. The two axes of the multiplier are **two different real symmetric forms evaluated on one ray**.

Two consequences follow immediately and neither is ours. If U commutes with C then Im a = 0, so a is real and, when a ≠ 0, arg a ∈ {0, π}; this is the finite-dimensional form of a statement recorded internally as ZS-S28 Theorems T and W, and externally, in the harder thermodynamic-limit form, as a conjecture of Ringel and Kovrizhin [B-8]. If Sym(Re U) is entrywise non-negative and nonzero then Re a > 0, because ψ₀ has strictly positive entries and the form cannot cancel; this is the finite-dimensional form of ZS-S28's quadrant bound. Both are cited, not claimed.

By Cauchy–Schwarz, |a| = 1 exactly when Uψ₀ ∥ ψ₀. So a phase carrier that stabilises the state the transfer operator selected produces no contraction.

### 3.2 Two aligned cases

Let K be generated by an orthonormal basis and let J ≠ ±I be a self-adjoint involution.

> **(a)** If J(K) ⊆ K and [P, J] = 0, then Jψ₀ is a top eigenvector lying in K, so by simplicity Jψ₀ = ψ₀, hence **Δ = 0 and i[J, σ₀] = 0**.
>
> **(b)** If J is diagonal in the generating basis, then [P, J]\_{jk} = P\_{jk}(ε_k − ε_j), so [P, J] = 0 would force P\_{jk} = 0 whenever ε_j ≠ ε_k, contradicting positivity improving. Hence **[P, J] ≠ 0 is forced**, and since every component of ψ₀ is strictly positive, Jψ₀ ≠ ±ψ₀, so **Δ ≠ 0**.

Both halves are elementary — (a) is the Perron–Frobenius uniqueness argument, (b) is the irreducibility of a strictly positive matrix. They are stated together because the pair is what does the work: the same question, "does the seam asymmetry of the selected state survive?", receives opposite answers according to how the grading sits relative to the cone. **The gate is an alignment question, not a commutator question.**

### 3.2.1 The two cases are not exhaustive

The predecessor called the pair a dichotomy (ALIGNMENT-CLASS-3) — a name that asserts exhaustiveness, and exhaustiveness is false, so the name is retired here and the companion's guard G11 fails if it returns undisclaimed (ALIGNMENT-CLASS-3).

> **Counterexample 2.** Let K be generated by an orthonormal basis in which
>
> > **P = [[2, 1], [1, 2]],  J = [[0, −1], [−1, 0]],  ψ₀ = (1, 1).**
>
> J is self-adjoint, J² = I and J ≠ ±I. P is entrywise positive, hence positivity improving, and Pψ₀ = 3ψ₀ with ψ₀ strictly positive, so ψ₀ is the Perron–Frobenius ray. But J is **not** diagonal in the generating basis, so (b) does not apply, and J does **not** preserve the cone — it sends the leading basis vector to minus the second — so (a) does not apply either. Here [P, J] = 0 and
>
> > **Jψ₀ = −ψ₀,  κ = −1,  T = √(1 − κ²) = 0.**

Three things follow. First, (a) and (b) do not cover the alignments: this instance sits outside both. Second, (a)'s conclusion fails in detail — the commutator vanishes as in (a), but the ray is a −1 eigenvector rather than a +1 eigenvector, because cone preservation was what fixed the sign. Third, and this is the one that matters, **the surviving half of Theorem 3.4 fails outright here**: positivity improving together with a non-central self-adjoint involution does *not* give |κ| < 1. The seam asymmetry is not merely small, it is zero.

So the hypothesis "diagonal in the generating basis" is not decoration and is not removable. Row N09 locks the failure as a family rather than an accident: every symmetric entrywise-positive operator with Perron ray (1, 1) has κ = −1 against this grading, 64 exact instances.

*Certificates:* rows A10, N08, N09, N13, and guard G11.

### 3.3 The asymmetry budget

With κ = ⟨ψ₀|J|ψ₀⟩ and ‖ψ₀‖ = 1,

> **Tr(Δ²) = 2(1 − κ²),  hence  T := ½‖Δ‖₁ = √(1 − κ²).**

The seam trace distance carried by the Perron–Frobenius ray is therefore fixed by one number. In particular a demand of the form T ≥ M is equivalent to |⟨ψ₀|J|ψ₀⟩| ≤ √(1 − M²) — a constraint on the transfer operator's leading eigenvector, not on the state space at large. Maximal asymmetry, κ = 0, holds exactly when the ray splits its mass evenly across the grading.

*Certificates:* rows G01–G07, D01–D04 of the companion, at dimensions 2 through 6, exact arithmetic.

### 3.4 What positivity supplies, and what it does not

This subsection replaces a statement of v1.0 and is the mathematical content of v1.1. Its hypothesis is the one §3.2.1 shows to be load-bearing.

**Withdrawn (ZS-M66 v1.0, abstract, §1 and §5.3).** *Because J is diagonal in the generating basis, case (b) applies to any positivity-improving P there, and the Perron–Frobenius ray therefore has strictly positive components in both eigenspaces, so the window 0 < |κ| < 1 holds automatically.* **WITHDRAWN-V1.1.** The conclusion does not follow: strictly positive components in both eigenspaces give |κ| < 1, and give nothing about κ = 0.

> **Theorem 3.4 (replacement).** Let K be generated by an orthonormal basis, let P be positivity improving on K, let J be a non-central involution **diagonal in that basis**, and let ψ₀ be the unit Perron–Frobenius ray. Then
>
> **(i)** |κ| < 1 strictly, hence Δ ≠ 0 and T = √(1 − κ²) > 0;
>
> **(ii)** κ = 0 is not excluded.
>
> Both parts are conditional on the diagonality hypothesis, which by §3.2.1 cannot be dropped.

*Proof of (i).* Write κ = Σ_j ε_j |ψ₀,j|². Every |ψ₀,j|² is strictly positive and both signs ε = ±1 occur because J is non-central, so κ is a strict convex combination of +1 and −1 and lies strictly inside (−1, 1). ∎

*Proof of (ii), by counterexample.*

> **P = [[2, 1], [1, 2]],  J = diag(−1, +1).**

P is entrywise positive, hence positivity improving. Pψ = 3ψ for ψ = (1, 1)/√2, and since ψ is strictly positive it is the Perron–Frobenius ray; the components are written unnormalised in the companion, where every quantity used is scale invariant, and normalised here, where the setting of §2 fixes ‖ψ₀‖ = 1. The commutator [P, J] = [[0, 2], [−2, 0]] is nonzero, as branch (b) requires, and Jψ ≠ ±ψ, so Δ ≠ 0. Yet

> **κ = (−1·1 + 1·1)/2 = 0,  T = √(1 − κ²) = 1.**

The seam asymmetry is at its **maximum**, and the multiplier of §5.3 becomes a(θ) = cos θ + i·0·sin θ = cos θ, which is real at every angle. The state is as seam-asymmetric as the model allows and carries no phase at all. ∎

> **Corollary 3.5.** *Positivity improving does not entail phase-capable.* Call a state **phase-capable** if Im a ≠ 0 for *some* angle with sin θ ≠ 0; the qualifier is needed, because Im a = κ sin θ dies at θ = 0 and θ = π whatever the state does, and a definition at one fixed angle would conflate a property of the state with a property of the boundary condition. With that definition, seam-asymmetry survival (Δ ≠ 0, equivalently T > 0) and phase capability are inequivalent conditions on the selected state, and they come apart exactly at the maximally asymmetric point T = 1. The condition the programme needs is therefore
>
> > **0 < |κ| < 1,**
>
> whose upper half is supplied by positivity *under the diagonal alignment* and whose lower half is not supplied by anything in this paper. Any candidate mechanism must select κ ≠ 0 separately. This is registered as gate **F-M66.11**. The alignment it assumes was carried by **F-M66.13** — now a retired gate superseded by F-M66.15 and F-M66.16 — so that assumption is a historical feature of the coordinate route rather than a live requirement.

**Why this matters more than a corrected sentence.** The imbalance κ = 0 is not a knife-edge accident. For symmetric 2×2 positivity-improving P the locus κ = 0 is exactly the equal-diagonal locus P₁₁ = P₂₂ — codimension one, and therefore invisible to any finite sample of witnesses that was not aimed at it. The v1.0 evidence for the withdrawn claim was a single positivity-improving matrix on which 0 < |κ| < 1 happened to hold. A witness passing is not a universal statement holding, and the companion now carries both counterexamples as locked regressions so that neither implication can return without a failing row.

### 3.6 The reachable set, in every dimension

The predecessor stated that the values of κ realised by positivity-improving operators fill (−1, 1), but proved it only in dimension two and only over positive rational data, so the certified statement was a dense subset while the printed statement was the interval. The gap closes with a shorter construction that does not use dimension two at all.

> **Theorem 3.6.** Let J be any non-central involution diagonal in the generating basis of K, in any finite dimension n ≥ 2. Then for every t ∈ (−1, 1) there is a positivity-improving P on K whose unit Perron–Frobenius ray ψ₀ satisfies ⟨ψ₀|J|ψ₀⟩ = t. One may take P positive definite and invertible.

*Proof.* Let ε_j = ±1 be the diagonal of J, with both signs occurring; write S₊ and S₋ for the two index sets. Given t, choose any strictly positive weights w_j with Σ_{S₊} w_j = (1 + t)/2 and Σ_{S₋} w_j = (1 − t)/2; both totals are strictly positive exactly because |t| < 1. Put ψ₀ = (√w_1, …, √w_n), which is strictly positive with ‖ψ₀‖ = 1, and set

> **P = ψ₀ψ₀ᵀ.**

Every entry of P is strictly positive, so P(K∖{0}) ⊂ int K. Pψ₀ = ψ₀, and since P has rank one its spectrum is {1, 0, …, 0}, so the top eigenvalue is simple and ψ₀ is the Perron–Frobenius ray. Then ⟨ψ₀|J|ψ₀⟩ = Σ_j ε_j w_j = t. For the second sentence replace P by P + εI with ε > 0: the entries stay strictly positive, the spectrum becomes {1 + ε, ε, …, ε}, the top eigenvalue is still simple, and the ray is unchanged. ∎

So under the diagonal alignment the constraint |κ| < 1 of Theorem 3.4(i) is exactly the whole constraint: nothing further about κ is implied by positivity, in any dimension. Read together with §3.2.1, the picture is that positivity supplies one strict inequality under one alignment and supplies nothing at all outside it.

### 3.7 The operator-state route, and a symmetry no-go

Everything above is stated relative to a cone generated by a chosen orthonormal basis, and §2.1 had to declare that choice on the fermion boundary space because nothing derives it. On a two-dimensional spinor space that cone is not natural. A more natural positive structure is the cone of positive semidefinite operators, with a positive trace-preserving map in place of a matrix acting on coordinates.

> **What this move is, stated before the results.** It is **not** a change of coordinates. Passing from a vector ψ₀ to a state ρ\*, and from a matrix P to a map Φ, is a lift from a vector-state model to an operator-state model. Once the carrier is fixed the cone is canonical and the basis choice of §2.1 does not arise — but **the carrier is itself a choice, and this paper does not derive it**. Nothing here shows that the boundary action selects a density-operator carrier, that the boundary dynamics induces a positive or completely positive trace-preserving map, that Tr(ρU) is the right multiplier for the record interpretation, or that a faithful fixed state is a boundary state rather than an effective description whose mixing comes from a coarse-graining. This is declared construction choice (7), it is registered in companion row Q11, and it is folded into gate F-M66.15. **The correct summary is that a basis-cone choice has been exchanged for a basis-independent carrier choice, not that a choice has been removed.**

**Setting.** Let 𝒜 = B(𝓗_∂) with 𝓗_∂ two-dimensional, let 𝒜₊ be its cone of positive semidefinite elements, let Φ : 𝒜 → 𝒜 be positive and trace preserving, and call Φ *primitive* if some power maps 𝒜₊∖{0} into the interior. Primitivity gives a single fixed state ρ\*, and that state is faithful; this is the Perron–Frobenius theorem for positive maps and it is imported [B-9, B-10, B-11]. Put

> **κ := Tr(ρ\* J_E),  and let X be the grading flip, the self-adjoint unitary with X J_E X = −J_E.**

> **Theorem 3.7.** If ρ\* is faithful then |κ| < 1 strictly.

*Proof.* In the eigenbasis of J_E the diagonal entries of ρ\* are strictly positive and sum to one, so κ is a strict convex combination of +1 and −1. ∎

The contrast with Theorem 3.4(i) is the point, and so is its price. There the strict bound needed the grading to be diagonal in the cone's generating basis, and §3.2.1 showed it is false without that hypothesis. Here the hypothesis is a property of the state — faithfulness — and no basis is chosen. Row Q10 makes the absence of *basis* freedom exact: rotating Φ and J_E together by any unitary leaves κ unchanged, so the alignment that produced counterexample 2 has no analogue.

**Where the bound comes from.** Faithfulness is not free. Row Q12 checks that on the diagonal data the operator-state multiplier and the vector expectation return the same number, so the lift does not change the quantity being bounded; but no pure state is faithful in dimension two or more, and the coordinate route of §3.4 lives entirely on pure states. So Theorem 3.7 does not obtain a stronger conclusion from weaker hypotheses — it obtains it from **mixing**, and where that mixing comes from is part of gate F-M66.15 rather than a result here. Row Q13 is the matching control: a pure fixed state has |κ| = 1 and T = 0, exactly the failure that alignment produced in the coordinate route. The improvement is in the *kind* of open question, not in the number of them.

> **Theorem 3.8 (the no-go).** If Φ is covariant under the grading flip, Φ∘Ad_X = Ad_X∘Φ, and Φ has a single fixed state, then κ = 0.

*Proof.* Covariance sends a fixed state to a fixed state: Φ(Xρ\*X) = XΦ(ρ\*)X = Xρ\*X. With only one fixed state, Xρ\*X = ρ\*. Then κ = Tr(ρ\*J_E) = Tr(Xρ\*X J_E) = Tr(ρ\* X J_E X) = −κ. ∎

This is the sharpest statement the line has produced about F-M66.11, and it changes what that gate asks for. Before it, the requirement was that some mechanism select κ ≠ 0, with no indication of what kind of mechanism. After it, the requirement is a **symmetry condition on the boundary map**: whatever map the action supplies must fail to commute with the sector exchange. No amount of tuning inside the covariant class helps, because the covariant class gives κ = 0 identically. Row Q07 records that the implication runs one way only: κ = 0 does not force covariance, so breaking the symmetry is necessary and not sufficient.

The no-go also has a quantitative form, which is what makes it usable as a design constraint rather than a slogan.

> **Theorem 3.9.** Let σ := Xρ\*X, let Φ^X := Ad_X∘Φ∘Ad_X, and let η < 1 be the contraction coefficient of Φ on traceless self-adjoint differences. Then
>
> > **2|κ| ≤ ‖ρ\* − σ‖₁ ≤ ‖Φ − Φ^X‖₁→₁ / (1 − η).**

*Proof.* The left inequality is |Tr((ρ\*−σ)J_E)| ≤ ‖ρ\*−σ‖₁ together with Tr(σJ_E) = −κ. For the second, σ is the fixed state of Φ^X, so ρ\*−σ = Φ(ρ\*) − Φ^X(σ) = [Φ(ρ\*) − Φ(σ)] + [(Φ − Φ^X)(σ)]; the difference ρ\*−σ is traceless, so the leading bracket has trace norm at most η‖ρ\*−σ‖₁, and rearranging gives the claim. ∎

Read as a requirement: a boundary map that is *nearly* flip covariant cannot select a usefully phase-capable state, and the programme's demand for a given |κ| translates into a lower bound on the map's flip defect. This is the same shape as the asymmetry-price statements the corpus already carries for boundary laws, one layer up, and it is the form in which F-M66.16 is stated.

The reachability statement carries over: the replacement channel ρ ↦ Tr(ρ)·diag((1−t)/2, (1+t)/2) is completely positive and trace preserving, its fixed state is faithful exactly when |t| < 1, and its κ equals t. So on this carrier positivity constrains κ by |κ| < 1 and by nothing else, without any hypothesis about a basis, and row Q04 exhibits an explicitly completely positive channel — Choi positivity certified by exact principal minors, not asserted — whose faithful fixed state has κ = 3/5.

**What this does and does not settle.** It retires the basis-alignment question — gate F-M66.13 is retired rather than narrowed, because on this carrier there is no generating basis to supply — and it does so *conditionally on the declared lift*, which is now the load-bearing assumption in its place. It replaces a vague selection demand with a symmetry condition carrying a numerical floor. It does not derive Φ from the action, it does not derive the lift, and it does not claim external mathematics: the Perron–Frobenius theory of positive maps is imported, the covariance argument is the standard one, and the perturbation bound is the standard one. What is ours is the mapping onto this programme's requirement, and §10.6 records that assessment together with the search limits behind it.

## §4. The explicit scalar boundary-term route closes negatively

Take the natural scalar candidate: the vacuum circle |Φ| = 1 of the Z-bias field, discretised, with a symmetric positive circulant transfer operator, and the reflection that the type lock of ZS-S14 v2.1 assigns to the field plane.

**The reflection preserves the cone of positive functions.** Case (a) of §3.2 therefore applies: the transfer operator commutes with it, the Perron–Frobenius ray is the constant function, Jψ₀ = ψ₀, and Δ = 0. Adding a **reflection-even** boundary weight changes nothing — the commutator still vanishes and every power iterate of a symmetric vector stays symmetric. Only a **reflection-breaking** weight opens the lock.

> **Type note, corrected in v1.1.** The v1.0 predecessor said "only an *odd* weight opens the lock". That names an empty class: a boundary weight is a strictly positive vector, and no strictly positive vector satisfies Jv = −v. The correct object is a positive weight whose decomposition into the ±1 eigenspaces of the reflection has a **nonzero odd component**. Row C09 exhibits the decomposition for both the opening and the non-opening weight.

Now the enumeration. Among the terms the printed list actually writes down, every one whose variation produces a scalar boundary term depends on H₅ only through |H₅|² or |∂_μH₅|², hence is even under the reflection. That includes the two kinetic terms, the non-minimal curvature coupling, and the Gibbons–Hawking–York term the coupling forces on a manifold with boundary, since it too carries |H₅|². The single term that breaks the covariance, the Yukawa, contains no derivative of H₅ and therefore contributes no scalar boundary term at all.

> **The explicit scalar boundary-term sector of ZS-S14 v2.1 cannot supply the reflection-breaking content that the seam asymmetry requires (SCALAR-SCOPE-V13).**

### 4.1 What this closure does not cover, corrected in this version

The predecessor stated the conclusion one quantifier too wide: it said every bosonic term of the printed list is reflection-even, and listed **the scalar potential V(H₅, H) among those terms**. The upstream action does not specify that potential. This paper knows it does not, because §6 below leans on exactly that fact: the CP⁴ direction of ⟨H₅⟩ is an unfixed debt supplying the mass phase, and a potential that fixed the symmetry would have to be written down before that debt could be described. The two statements cannot both stand, and the one that goes is the wider quantifier.

So the closure divides.

> **CERTIFIED, in scope.** The derivative-generated scalar boundary terms that ZS-S14 v2.1 writes down are reflection-even, and the Yukawa contributes no scalar boundary term. The direct route — read a reflection-breaking boundary weight off an explicit term — is closed over that list.
>
> **OPEN.** Whether an unspecified potential closes the sector as a whole. A potential may select a vacuum that breaks the reflection, and the induced effective boundary weight or transfer structure would then be asymmetric without any single printed term being odd. Row C11 makes the distinction concrete: a strictly positive weight with a nonzero odd component exists on this very cone and does open the lock, so the question is which weight the vacuum selects, and absence of specification is not evenness. This is gate **F-M66.14**.

Rows C07 and C08 are retyped from class C to class W accordingly: the computations are unchanged and correct, but each was cited for a sentence quantified over a list whose potential is unspecified, and a computation over the terms one can write down is a witness. This is the third time in three versions that a row checking what we could write was read as a statement about everything there is; the pattern is recorded as a do-not-repeat in the companion.

Emission of the narrowed closure was checked against the programme's no-go gate; the record is in companion row R07, and the quantifier now printed there is the enumeration over written terms rather than over the sector.

## §5. The fermion boundary sector

### 5.1 What the Dirac term gives without being asked

Varying ψ̄ iγ^μ D_μ ψ leaves the boundary sesquilinear form ω(ψ, χ) = ψ†Nχ with N = γ⁰γ^n. Then N† = N, N² = I and tr N = 0, so ω has signature (2,2).

**N commutes with γ₅.** The boundary form is chirality *diagonal* — it is the normal vector current, which preserves chirality. (The chirality-flipping bilinear is the mass term, not the flux. Our own earlier expectation was the opposite and was corrected by this computation; the record is in companion row R05, item C1.)

Consequently the +1 eigenspace E₊ is two-dimensional and contains one left-handed and one right-handed component, so

> **J_E := γ₅|_{E₊} = diag(−1, +1) is a non-central self-adjoint involution on a two-dimensional space.**

This single object meets the three requirements ZS-M56 places on the environment: dimension two, a non-central involution, and residence outside the Z-register. It is not inserted; it is what the first-order term leaves behind. **It is also entirely imported** — the boundary structure of the Dirac operator and its chirality decomposition are standard, and the corpus gate that asks whether any two-dimensional factor carries a non-central involution is therefore answered by a borrowed object rather than a new one.

What the term does *not* leave behind is a cone. There is no transfer operator on E₊ in the printed action, so there is no positive structure and no generating basis; §2.1 records the identification of that basis with the chirality eigenbasis as a declared choice.

### 5.2 Boundary conditions and the boundary unitary

Self-adjoint boundary conditions are the maximal isotropic subspaces of ω, equivalently the graphs of unitaries E₊ → E₋; the moduli space is U(2) [B-7]. The chiral-bag family [B-1]

> M_θ = −i γ^n e^{iθγ₅},  ψ = M_θ ψ

is a U(1) inside it: M_θ is a Hermitian involution with vanishing trace and anticommutes with N, so its range is maximal isotropic. Identifying E₊ with E₋ by γ⁰ and normalising so that θ = 0 gives the identity, the induced unitary on the boundary space is

> **U_θ = e^{iθ J_E}.**

The grading generates the chosen U(1) parametrisation. It does not generate the angle: θ is a coordinate on that subfamily and is not derived here, and the subfamily itself was selected from the U(2) moduli rather than derived. Both the family and this parametrisation are imported; see §8.

### 5.3 The multiplier, and the factorisation

For a boundary state with grading imbalance κ,

> **a(θ) = cos θ + i κ sin θ,   1 − |a|² = (1 − κ²) sin²θ = T² sin²θ.**

The contraction deficit factorises into the state's contribution T and the boundary condition's contribution sin θ. Reading the axes:

| axis | carried by | vanishes when |
|---|---|---|
| Re a = cos θ | the boundary condition alone | — |
| Im a = κ sin θ | a **product** | κ = 0 or sin θ = 0 |
| 1 − \|a\|² = T² sin²θ | a **product** | T = 0 or sin θ = 0 |

Since κ² + T² = 1, a nonzero imaginary part needs κ ≠ 0 and sin θ ≠ 0, while a strict contraction needs |κ| < 1 and the same sin θ ≠ 0. Together: **0 < |κ| < 1 with sin θ ≠ 0**, the state partially but not fully polarised and the boundary condition away from the two degenerate angles. The predecessor's version of this sentence carried only the κ factor.

**That window is an assumption, not a consequence.** WITHDRAWN-V1.1: the v1.0 predecessor argued here that because J_E is diagonal in the boundary basis, case (b) of §3.2 applies to any positivity-improving P on that space, so the window holds automatically. Theorem 3.4 shows what actually follows is the upper half only, and §3.2.1 shows that even the upper half needs the boundary basis to be the cone's generating basis. On the boundary space the counterexample of §3.4 is realised directly: the transfer operator [[2, 1], [1, 2]] is a legitimate positivity-improving operator there, its Perron–Frobenius ray is (1, 1), the seam asymmetry is maximal, and a(θ) = cos θ is real at every angle. **The alignment that shut the vacuum circle does not open this one.** It removes one of the two obstacles and leaves the other standing, and the one it leaves standing is the one the programme needs.

An exact rational instance in which the window does hold: cos θ = −3/5, ψ₀ ∝ (1, 2) giving κ = 3/5, so a = −3/5 + 12i/25 with |a|² = 369/625 — second quadrant, strictly contractive, no locked constant used anywhere in its construction.

> **What the instance shows and does not show.** The pair (θ, κ) reaches every point of the open unit disc: cos θ covers (−1, 1) and κ sin θ covers (−|sin θ|, |sin θ|). The instance therefore demonstrates that the class is nonempty and demonstrates **nothing** about selection. Matching a two-real-parameter target with two free real parameters is identification, not derivation, and we state this as a non-claim rather than a caveat.

*Certificates:* rows D06–D19, N01–N13; adversarial sweep over nine exact angles and 200 states each, zero counterexamples to the factorisation.

### 5.4 What leaks

The sharpest objection to §5.3 is that the angle might carry no observable content, because in the bag-model literature the Cheshire Cat Principle compensates interior dependence against an exterior field [B-6]. That compensation is an anomaly-inflow statement about a **pair**: the exterior absorbs exactly what leaves the interior. So the objection is decided by computing what leaves.

On the boundary subspace, at seven exact angles including θ = 0 and θ = π:

> **the vector flux vanishes identically** — ψ†Nχ = 0, which is the isotropy condition itself, so nothing charged leaks and the boundary confines;
> **the axial flux does not** — N γ₅ restricts, in the graph basis, to diag(−1, +1), the same matrix for every angle.

The two fluxes are different objects on the same subspace even though N and γ₅ commute. And the restricted axial flux **is** the grading J_E: the odd resource the programme demands is the axial boundary flux of the confined fermion. That bag boundary conditions break the axial symmetry is the oldest known fact about them [B-1, B-3] and is cited, not claimed.

The verdict must be stated in two pieces, because the v1.0 predecessor ran them together.

> **CERTIFIED, in scope.** ZS-S14 v2.1 declares no exterior field. The truncated action therefore contains no explicit exterior sector implementing the standard Cheshire-Cat compensation, and the boundary carries an axial flux that the printed term list does not absorb.
>
> **OPEN.** Whether the angle is consequently observable in the full quantum theory. The exact computation above is a local statement about a truncated classical boundary form; anomaly leakage and compensating surface terms are long-established features of this class of model, and ruling out a compensating mechanism in the completed theory requires the anomaly computation that gate F-M66.5 names. The v1.0 predecessor concluded observability directly from the absence of a term in the list, which over-reads a local flux result.

What the computation does leave behind is exact and is an obligation upstream. Either the action supplies a field to absorb the flux — and then the angle is imported from that field rather than derived — or the axial symmetry is explicitly broken at the boundary, which is a declared physical input. Neither branch yields a derived phase. This is recorded as gate F-M66.8, re-typed and still open.

*Certificates:* rows E01–E08, seven angles.

## §6. The residue is one angle, inside an externally selected subfamily

Under a chiral rotation ψ → e^{iβγ₅}ψ the boundary condition maps M_θ ↦ M_{θ−2β} while the Yukawa mass phase maps θ_m ↦ θ_m + 2β. Only

> **Δ := θ + θ_m**

is invariant under that reparametrisation.

> **Scope, tightened in v1.1.** The statement above is a theorem *within the reduced free-Dirac boundary model*, where the chiral rotation is treated as a classical reparametrisation of the boundary data. The v1.0 predecessor called Δ "the physical datum" without qualification. That is too strong. In a gauge theory the quantum chiral rotation is anomalous and shifts the vacuum angle as well, and the physical invariant of the standard treatment is a combination of the vacuum angle with the mass phases. Which combination plays that role here is exactly what gate **F-M66.5** leaves open, and F-M66.5 is load-bearing. The closest external precedent, in two dimensions, relates a fermion boundary condition to the θ angle directly [B-5]; that relation has not been transported to the present setting.

ZS-S14 v2.1 fixes neither summand. θ is a coordinate on the boundary moduli space, and self-adjointness alone leaves a manifold rather than a point. θ_m is the chiral phase of the Yukawa mass y₀⟨H⟩(T·⟨H₅⟩); the overall scale is derived and the invariant tensor is multiplicity-one, so the phase is a function of the direction of ⟨H₅⟩ in CP⁴ — the eight unfixed real parameters that the corrected action itself registers as a debt.

> **Within the externally selected chiral-bag U(1) subfamily, the remaining boundary parameter is one real angle, one of whose two summands is an already-registered debt.**

> **Scope, tightened in this version.** The predecessor called this a reduction of an infinite-dimensional debt to one angle. It is not. §5.2 records that the full moduli space of self-adjoint boundary conditions is U(2), and the chiral-bag U(1) inside it was chosen, not derived. So the honest statement is a **conditional parametrisation**: given that subfamily, one angle remains. The missing arrow, U(2) → U(1), is gate **F-M66.17**, and until it is answered no debt has been reduced — the parameters have been organised, and the organisation is conditional on a choice.

This is a parametrisation, not a closure, and its scope is the chosen subfamily. The precision gain is of the same kind that an earlier paper in this line achieved by replacing "derive the boundary phase law" with a scalar inequality; here the scalar acquires a candidate physical identity as well, subject to F-M66.5.

### 10.6 Where the value of §3.7 comes from, and where it does not

The four repairs above make the paper more accurate; they do not make it worth more. §3.7 is where the value is, and it is worth being precise about what kind of value it is.

**It exchanges a choice for a better-typed one.** Two versions ago the audit found that the cone on the boundary space was an undeclared assumption; the response was to declare it, open a gate, and add a non-claim. §3.7 does not remove that assumption — it replaces it. What is gained is real but bounded: κ is frame independent, the third alignment class has no analogue, and a formulation in which an embarrassing counterexample cannot be stated is better than one in which it must be guarded against. What is not gained is a reduction in the number of undeclared objects, since the carrier lift is one, and §3.7 now says so in its own opening rather than in a later qualification.

**It converts a wish into a testable condition.** F-M66.11 asked that something select κ ≠ 0. That is not a research target, it is a restatement of the problem. Theorem 3.8 replaces it with: the boundary map must not commute with the sector exchange. That is a property of a candidate object, checkable the moment a candidate exists, and Theorem 3.9 attaches a number to it. The kill test in §7 is the same statement read the other way, and it can fail — which is what makes it worth registering.

**It does not move the frontier.** No map is derived from the action and no lift is either. RQ3 is OPEN before §3.7 and OPEN after it, exactly as before, and it is OPEN at the proposed freeze. The gates that replaced F-M66.13 are, if anything, harder to satisfy than it was, because they ask for two objects rather than for a declaration.

**External mathematical novelty remains zero, and this is not modesty.** The Perron–Frobenius theorem for positive maps is standard [B-9, B-10, B-11]; the argument that covariance plus a single fixed point forces invariance is the standard averaging argument; the bound of Theorem 3.9 is the standard fixed-point perturbation estimate with a contraction coefficient. What this section contributes is the identification of κ with the programme's phase-capability requirement and the consequent typing of that requirement as symmetry breaking. That is corpus-internal value. Search limits are recorded in companion row R28: four query families on quantum Perron–Frobenius and primitive channels, no reading of the bodies, and no claim that absence of a hit is absence of prior art.

### 10.7 The proposed freeze, and what it does not mean

ZS-M66 is **proposed for freeze at v1.5.1**, terminal in scope for the scope stated in §7, as a SUPPORT / METHOD technical note. **The freeze is not recorded as taken.** The predecessor recorded it, and its approval, as accomplished facts; that was a release-metadata defect and it is withdrawn. The freeze becomes a decision only after a delta re-audit of the three corrections in §10.2e passes and an approval follows it.

**What that does not mean, once granted.** It would not be REVIEW READY. It has had no qualified-human review and no deterministic formalisation. Six audit rounds each returned substantive findings, five of the six returned findings no passing run could see — the sixth round found one in a guard the fifth had added for exactly that purpose — and the honest prior is that a seventh would find more. Freezing is a decision about where to spend, not a certificate about the artifact.

**What carries forward.** Positivity does not select a phase-capable state. The coordinate formulation has an alignment freedom that no action fixes. On an operator-state carrier the sector-exchange symmetry forces κ = 0 exactly, so the requirement is symmetry breaking with a quantitative floor. The open objects are the boundary map, the lift that gives it a carrier, and the selection of the chiral-bag subfamily from the full moduli.

**Handoff, on the same condition.** The successor is a construction paper — no identifier is assigned here, and none is reserved — that builds a boundary positive or completely positive trace-preserving map from the Z-Spin boundary action together with the operator-state lift that carries it, and then applies the pre-registered kill test of §7. If the constructed map commutes with the sector exchange, Theorem 3.8 gives κ = 0 and the boundary-phase route ends. If it does not commute, Theorem 3.9 converts the required imbalance into a lower bound on the flip defect. If no map follows from the action at all, the construction has failed, and that is also a result.
