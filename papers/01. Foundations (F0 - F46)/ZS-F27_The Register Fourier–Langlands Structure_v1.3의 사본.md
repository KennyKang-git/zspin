**Theorem 3.3 (Seam Factorization). \[PROVEN\]**  The corpus seam involution satisfies

J  \=  F²S  \=  S⁻¹F² ,      and      F² S F² \= S⁻¹ .

*Proof.* F²S|j⟩ \= F²|j+1⟩ \= |−(j+1) mod 11⟩ \= |10 − j⟩ \= J|j⟩ for every j \= 0, …, 10\. The second form follows since parity conjugates the shift to its inverse: F²SF² \= PSP \= S⁻¹, so S⁻¹F² \= F²S. Involutivity is then structural: J² \= F²SF²S \= S⁻¹S \= I. □ (Verified: checks C1–C3, A2.)

**§3.5  Seam-odd uncertainty on prime cyclic groups**

**Lemma 3.8 (seam-odd structure). \[PROVEN\]**  Let N be odd, f ∈ ℂ^N nonzero with J\_N f \= −f (seam-odd; here (J\_N f)(k) \= f(N−1−k)). Then: (i) f((N−1)/2) \= 0, supp f is a disjoint union of seam pairs {k, N−1−k}, and |supp f| is even, ≥ 2; (ii) under the §3.1 convention, f̂(−m) \= −ω^m f̂(m) for all m; hence f̂(0) \= 0, supp f̂ is paired under m ↦ −m, and |supp f̂| is even with supp f̂ ⊆ ℤ\_N ∖ {0}. *Proof.* (i) Immediate from f(N−1−k) \= −f(k): the center is the unique fixed point of the seam reflection. (ii) Substituting k ↦ N−1−k in f̂(−m) \= Σ\_k f(k)ω^{−km} and using −(N−1−k) ≡ k+1 (mod N) gives f̂(−m) \= −ω^m f̂(m); at m \= 0 this forces f̂(0) \= 0, and for odd N the involution m ↦ −m has 0 as its only fixed point. □ (Verified: check U1.)

**Theorem 3.9 (Seam-Odd Uncertainty Theorem). \[PROVEN\]**  Let p be an odd prime and f ∈ ℂ^p nonzero with J\_p f \= −f. Then

|supp f| · |supp f̂|  ≥  2(p − 1\) ,

with equality if and only if f is a seam dipole f \= c(δ\_k − δ\_{p−1−k}), k ≠ (p−1)/2, or f̂ is a dual dipole supported on {m, −m}, m ≠ 0, with f̂(−m) \= −ω^m f̂(m) — in the latter case f(k) \= c·ω^{−km}(1 − ω^{(2k+1)m}), a discrete wave vanishing exactly at the center slot. *Proof.* Write a \= |supp f|, b \= |supp f̂|. By Lemma 3.8, a and b are even, a ≥ 2, and (center, respectively 0, excluded) a ≤ p−1, b ≤ p−1. By Tao’s uncertainty principle for prime cyclic groups \[17\], a \+ b ≥ p \+ 1\. For fixed even a the minimum admissible b is max(2, p+1−a), so ab ≥ a(p+1−a), a concave quadratic in a minimized on the even range \[2, p−1\] at its endpoints, where it equals 2(p−1); interior even values a ≥ 4 give ab ≥ 4(p−3) \> 2(p−1) for p \> 5, and the cases p \= 3, 5 are checked directly. Hence ab ≥ 2(p−1), and equality forces {a, b} \= {2, p−1}. If a \= 2, the pairing of Lemma 3.8(i) forces the dipole form, and f̂(m) \= cω^{km}(1 − ω^{(p−1−2k)m}) vanishes iff (p−1−2k)m ≡ 0 (mod p), i.e. iff m \= 0 since p−1−2k ≢ 0; so b \= p−1 exactly. If b \= 2, Lemma 3.8(ii) forces the dual-dipole form, and the displayed f vanishes iff (2k+1)m ≡ 0 (mod p), i.e. only at k \= (p−1)/2; so a \= p−1 exactly. □ (Verified: checks U2–U3, with the minimum attained exactly at 2(p−1) for p ∈ {5, 7, 11}.)

**Appendix B.  Closure Computation for Theorem 4.2(iii)**
