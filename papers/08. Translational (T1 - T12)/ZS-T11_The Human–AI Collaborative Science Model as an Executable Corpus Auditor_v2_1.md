### **§5.2 The Four Search Modalities**

Within Phases 1 and 2, four search modalities are deployed depending on the character of the open problem:

**§5.2.1 Free Exploration**

Trigger: a gap is open and the solution space is uncharted. The AI is given the open problem statement and the relevant corpus theorems, and asked to brainstorm 5–7 candidate closure paths without being steered toward any particular approach. The author scores each candidate against: (a) corpus consistency, (b) zero free parameter constraint, (c) anti-numerology pre-registration possibility. The bottom 2–4 candidates are dropped; the remainder enter Phase 2\.

Key rule: **hallucination prohibition**. The author's instruction set for free exploration explicitly forbids the AI from predicting a result and then constructing a post-hoc justification. If the AI offers a result without a derivation chain, it is automatically placed at OBSERVATION status pending full derivation.

**§5.2.2 Deep Dive (Issue-Tree Search)**

Trigger: a candidate path from Free Exploration has structural support but is not yet closed. The problem is decomposed using a MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive) issue tree. Each node of the tree is assigned an epistemic status (PROVEN / DERIVED / HYPOTHESIS-strong / OPEN) and the tree is traversed in dependency order. Convergence is measured by the number of nodes that change status between successive traversal cycles — analogous to the Lyapunov condition |f′(z\*)| \< 1 from ZS-M1. If more nodes change status in cycle k+1 than in cycle k, the decomposition is re-examined.

**§5.2.3 Corpus Search**

Trigger: a result is suspected to follow from prior corpus theorems but the connection is not explicit. The full corpus index (The Book v5.0) is searched for every theorem, lemma, or bridge that mentions the relevant objects. This search is exhaustive — partial or sample search is prohibited. The result is a dependency list that either closes the connection or registers it as OPEN.

**§5.2.4 External Literature Search**

Trigger: the corpus search fails to close a connection, or a result is suspected to be prior art. External arXiv and journal literature is searched (via Claude's web search and the author's domain knowledge) for PROVEN external results that can be imported as PROVEN inputs, relieving the corpus of the burden of re-proving them. Every external import is cited in APS style and assigned the status IMPORTED-PROVEN.

The four search modalities are not stylistic preferences. They are different epistemic operations and must be selected by trigger condition.

The HACSM runs on a 14-status epistemic vocabulary that is applied consistently across all 121 papers. ### **§5.5 v1.1 Additive Update — Recursive Self-Audit Protocol**

The most important anti-hallucination mechanism in HACSM is not simply Monte Carlo, Python verification, or multiple AI tools. It is the requirement that every claim be turned back upon itself as an object of audit. HACSM therefore uses a **recursive self-audit protocol**.

1\. **Claim extraction.** Extract C as a standalone sentence with its mathematical objects, numerical values, status tag, and dependency list. 2\. **Reverse dependency read.** Trace every input of C back to The Book and then to the source paper. This protocol makes self-reference operational. The AI is not merely asked to produce a claim; it is forced to inspect the conditions under which its own claim would fail. This import contract is especially important for AI collaboration because LLMs are prone to analogy inflation. They often treat resemblance as proof. HACSM forbids that move: resemblance may trigger External Search, but only condition matching can produce an import.

## **§6. Why the Z-Spin Corpus Was Possible: Structural Analysis**

### **§6.1 The Constraint Density Advantage**

Highly constrained theoretical frameworks are particularly well-suited to the HACSM because the constraint density acts as a natural falsifier for AI hallucinations. In Z-Spin Cosmology, every new claim must be consistent with A \= 35/437 (LOCKED), Q \= 11 (PROVEN), (Z,X,Y) \= (2,3,6) (PROVEN), and all prior DERIVED theorems. This means that a hallucinated result — one that is plausible-sounding but mathematically incorrect — is quickly detected by the verification suite. The corpus essentially provides its own immune system against AI confabulation.

Frameworks with more free parameters are more susceptible to AI-assisted numerology: it is easy for an AI to propose a formula with several adjustable inputs that matches existing data. The zero free parameter constraint eliminates this failure mode entirely.

### **§6.2 The Scale–Coherence Trade-off**

Conventional single-author theoretical physics operates at a scale of 5–20 papers per year, each undergoing months of internal development before submission. The HACSM operates at a scale of 5–10 papers per week, because the AI handles the most time-consuming steps: drafting, symbolic manipulation, code generation, and preliminary cross-referencing. The author's time is concentrated on the tasks that require irreplaceable human judgment: epistemic status decisions, falsification recognition, and corpus coherence maintenance.

This is not a speed-quality trade-off. The verification suite provides objective quality control that is at least as rigorous as conventional peer review — and in several respects more so, since it is applied uniformly to every claim rather than selectively. The anti-numerology Monte Carlo is not a standard feature of any major physics journal's review process.

### **§6.3 The Memory Architecture Compensation**

AI language models do not have persistent memory across conversations. In a multi-paper programme, this is a severe limitation: the AI cannot recall, in session 47, what was decided in session 3\. The Z-Spin corpus compensates for this architectural limitation through two mechanisms: (a) The Book of Z-Spin Cosmology — a human-maintained navigation index updated after each new paper, which is provided as context at the start of each new session; and (b) the Locked Inputs table in every paper, which makes all relevant prior results explicit at the beginning of each derivation.

These mechanisms convert the AI's stateless architecture from a bug into a feature: every session is forced to be self-contained, which means every paper is forced to be self-contained. This is precisely the condition required for external reproducibility.

**v1.1 Additive Clarification.** The memory architecture compensation is now understood as an OS-level architecture. The Book v6.0 does not merely remind the AI of prior papers; it supplies the executable context in which the AI can reason without corrupting the corpus. In this sense, HACSM has four interacting layers: (1) the human author as integrator and final judge; (2) AI systems as reasoning processors; (3) verification suites as test runners; and (4) The Book as the persistent operating system and dependency resolver.

### **§6.4 The Role of Intellectual Honesty as a Technical Requirement**

The HACSM requires intellectual honesty not as a virtue but as a \*technical requirement\*. A researcher who rationalises around a failed verification, or fails to register a RETRACTION when a result is falsified, does not merely compromise the ethics of the programme — they destroy its navigational utility. The progressive-falsification engine relies on the accurate recording of failures. The corpus relies on the accurate recording of epistemic status. If either is corrupted, the cross-paper consistency guarantees break down.

This is why the HACSM is not simply 'use Claude to write physics papers.' The discipline — epistemic tagging, no-deletion, pre-registration of falsification gates, anti-numerology MC — is what converts AI-assisted writing into AI-assisted science.
