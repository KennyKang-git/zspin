# **§4. Theorem M49.NG1 — the acceptance window cannot prune**

**Theorem M49.NG1 \[PROVEN\].** P := {m log p : p prime, m ≥ 1} ⊂ ℝ is not uniformly discrete.

*Proof.* By the prime number theorem p\_{n+1}/p\_n → 1, hence log p\_{n+1} − log p\_n → 0\.

**Corollary M49.NG1a \[PROVEN\].** P is not a model set arising from a **relatively compact** acceptance window. Every regular cut-and-project set — lattice 𝓛 ⊂ ℝ^d × H, internal space H any locally compact abelian group, window W ⊂ H relatively compact with non-empty interior — is Delone, hence uniformly discrete. Therefore no such window construction, in **any** internal space (archimedean ℝ³, non-archimedean ℤ\_p, or adelic), has P as its point set.

**Verification (E1–E4).** Below 10⁶ the minimum log-gap is 2.000 × 10⁻⁶; restricted to p \< 10⁴ it is 2.014 × 10⁻⁴. The gap decreases with the window, as the theorem requires.

**External corroboration \[IMPORTED-PROVEN\].** Lev–Olevskii \[7, 8\]: a crystalline measure on ℝ with both support and spectrum uniformly discrete is periodic. The measure μ\_Λ, whose Fourier transform sits on the zeros by the explicit formula, therefore cannot be a quasicrystal in the strict sense unless periodic. NG1 exhibits which side fails: **the support**.

**Consequence, and its exact scope \[v1.2\].** The icosahedral cut-and-project structure is a **character carrier**, not a **pruning mechanism**. What is excluded is the *relatively-compact-window* route. Nothing here excludes unbounded windows, non-Delone constructions, or dynamical systems that are not model sets at all. See §5.1.

# **§5. Theorem M49.NG2 — no finite-type, finite-valued roof**

**Theorem M49.NG2 \[DERIVED — NO-GO\].** Let φ be a suspension flow over a subshift of finite type, or more generally over any system whose roof function is **locally constant on a finite Markov partition** (hence takes finitely many values). Then the group generated by its primitive orbit lengths lies in the ℤ-module spanned by those finitely many values, and is finitely generated. But {log p : p prime} is ℚ-linearly independent (unique factorisation), so it generates a free abelian group of infinite rank. No such flow realises POME.

## **7.3 The two conventions, named**

**P-TRANS \[CONVENTION\].** ν(Γ\_𝔭) \= 1: the transverse measure is the normalised Haar measure of the compact transverse group.

# **§9. Theorem M49.C — the theta–Lipschitz zero-exclusion certificate**

## **9.1 The theorem**

Let χ be a real primitive Dirichlet character mod q, parity a ∈ {0, 1}, root number ε(χ) \= \+1 (automatic for real primitive χ). Write α := a/2 − 3/4 \< 0 and

*θ\_χ(x) := Σ\_{n≥1} n^a χ(n) e^{−πn²x/q},   Θ(x) := Σ\_{n≥1} n^a e^{−πn²x/q} ≥ |θ\_χ(x)|.*

**Theorem M49.C \[PROVEN\].**

**(a) (Reality and the theta form.)** From the Hecke functional equation θ\_χ(1/x) \= x^{a+½} θ\_χ(x) and ε(χ) \= \+1,

*Λ(½ \+ it, χ) \= 2 ∫₁^∞ θ\_χ(x) · x^{α} · cos((t/2)·log x) · dx,*

which is manifestly **real** for real t.

**(b) (A t-independent Lipschitz constant.)** Differentiating under the integral (justified by absolute convergence),

*∂\_t Λ(½ \+ it, χ) \= − ∫₁^∞ θ\_χ(x) · x^{α} · (log x) · sin((t/2)·log x) · dx,*

hence for **every** real t,

*| ∂\_t Λ(½ \+ it, χ) | ≤ M₁(χ) := ∫₁^∞ Θ(x) · x^{α} · log x · dx.*

**(c) (Zero exclusion.)** If 0 \= t₀ \< t₁ \< … \< t\_N \= T is a partition with cells h\_k := t\_{k+1} − t\_k and

*| Λ(½ \+ i t\_k, χ) | \> M₁(χ) · h\_k   for every k,*

then Λ(½ \+ it, χ) ≠ 0 for **every** t ∈ \[0, T\]. In particular **no zero of any multiplicity** — not merely no sign change — lies on the critical line below T.

**Lemma M49.C1 \[PROVEN — a closed-form bound, no quadrature\].** Since α \< 0 gives x^α ≤ 1 on \[1, ∞), and log x ≤ x − 1 there,

*M₁(χ) ≤ Σ\_{n≥1} n^a ∫₁^∞ e^{−πn²x/q}(x − 1\) dx \= **(q/π)² · Σ\_{n≥1} n^{a−4} · e^{−πn²/q}**.*

The closed form is loose (a factor ≈ 2 at q \= 3, ≈ 130 at q \= 165\) but is **entirely quadrature-free**, so it is available as an independent safety net; check G17 confirms by **interval comparison** that the sharp, rigorously computed M₁ respects it in every channel.

**Lemma M49.C2 (the two truncation tails, in closed form) \[PROVEN — new in v1.3\].** Put c := π/q and let N, U be positive integers with cN² ≥ 60 and c·e^U ≥ 60\. Write Θ\_N for the theta majorant truncated at n \= N, and X := e^U. Then:

**(a) (n-tail, no truncation.)** For every x ≥ 1, Σ\_{n\>N} n^a e^{−cn²x} ≤ S\_N · e^{−cN²x} with

*S\_N := Σ\_{n\>N} n^a e^{−c(n²−N²)} ≤ **q / 2π**,*

the bound holding whenever N ≥ √(q/2π) (which cN² ≥ 60 already implies), by comparison of Σ\_{n\>N} n e^{−c(n²−N²)} with e^{cN²}∫\_N^∞ x e^{−cx²}dx \= 1/(2c). Hence, using x^α ≤ 1 and log x ≤ x − 1 on \[1, ∞), the n-tail contributes at most **S\_N · e^{−cN²} / (cN²)²**.

**(b) (x-tail, with no monotonicity assumption.)** For u ≥ U, Θ(e^u) ≤ B · e^{−c·e^u} with

*B := Σ\_{n≥1} n^a e^{−c(n²−1)X} ≤ **1 \+ 1/(2cX)**,*

and, using **only** x^α ≤ X^α (valid since α \< 0\) and log x ≤ x,

*∫\_X^∞ e^{−cx} x^α log x dx ≤ **X^α · e^{−cX} · (X/c \+ 1/c²)**.*

Part (b) is the repair of a real error. v1.2 bounded the x-tail by asserting that x^α log x is decreasing beyond X. For **even** parity (α \= −3/4) it is, beyond x \= e^{4/3} ≈ 3.79. ## **10.3 Conjecture M49.FL — downgraded, and its hypotheses named \[v1.2\]**

## **11.1 A preconditioner selection rule for finite Weil / Suzuki audits**

Suzuki's finite-interval operator \[14\] and the Weil quadratic form give a certified sign at test frequency t and Gaussian width a only after summing over primes up to N\*, and ZS-M48's threshold law T9 says

*log N\*(a, t, γ) \= |γ − t|/a \+ 1/(2a) \+ O(1/|γ − t|),*

so the **cost is linear in the distance from the test frequency to the nearest usable zero**. For an abelian field F/ℚ whose Dedekind zeta factors into real Dirichlet channels, one is therefore free to *choose the cheapest channel*. The recipe:

1. **Enumerate** the real primitive characters of F (for multiquadratic F: the genus characters, conductors \= the products of the ramified primes).  
2. **Certify** min\_χ γ₁(χ) by Theorem M49.C — cost per channel: one rigorous quadrature for M₁ (O(√q) theta terms) plus a few hundred ball evaluations of Λ. Every channel in this paper certifies in under 4 seconds.  
3. **Precondition**: run the audit in the argmin channel. The budget is log N\* \= γ₁^min/a \+ 1/(2a).  
4. **Enlarge if it is cheap to do so.** By Lemma M49.O1 the minimum never rises; empirically (§10.2) it strictly falls in 86% of cases, with a median saving of 1.5 orders of magnitude in N\*, and the largest conductor is the argmin 59% of the time.

## **11.3 What Theorem M49.C is, and — precisely — what it is not \[v1.3\]**

**We claim no novelty over rigorous large-scale L-zero verification.** Platt's work already provides rigorous, Turing-method-based computation and verification of zeros of Dirichlet L-functions on a very large scale — GRH is verified for all primitive characters of modulus q ≤ 400 000 up to substantial heights \[24, 25\]. Anyone needing zeros at height, in bulk, or to high precision should use those methods, not this one. v1.2's sentence "we are not aware of a packaged tool that does this" was written without a literature search and is **withdrawn**.

The correct, narrower, and defensible position:

Theorem M49.C is **not** a replacement for Turing-type or Platt-type rigorous L-zero verification. It is a **lightweight, low-height, per-character certificate**: it establishes, in seconds and in a dozen lines of interval arithmetic, that the *first* critical-line zero of a real primitive Dirichlet L-function is where it appears to be **and that nothing of any multiplicity lies below it** — which is exactly, and only, what channel selection for a Suzuki/Weil audit requires.

Its virtues are its narrowness: no Turing method, no Riemann–Siegel/Rudnick–Sarnak machinery, no FFT, no zero-counting formula, no smoothing. It needs a theta series with O(√q) terms, one rigorous quadrature, and a few hundred ball evaluations. Its limitation is the same narrowness: the covering grid costs O(M₁/|Λ′(γ₁)|·log(1/ε)) cells, which is fine at the bottom of the critical strip and hopeless at height.

*A note for readers of the Katz–Sarnak literature.* The certified quantity γ₁·log q / 2π is exactly the scaled lowest zero whose distribution the symplectic hard-edge law predicts, and M49.C makes it rigorously computable per character with the even-multiplicity case **excluded rather than assumed away**. Extending the table to all real primitive χ with q ≤ 10⁴ is straightforward (**OPEN-M49.2**).

# **§13. Conclusion**

Three things are established, one is withdrawn, and one open question is sharper than when the paper began.

**Established, and usable by anyone: Theorem M49.C.** From the Hecke theta representation, the completed L-function of a real primitive Dirichlet character has a **t-independent Lipschitz constant**, computable rigorously and cheaply — with a quadrature-free closed form as a safety net. A covering grid then excludes zeros of **every** multiplicity below a given height, in seconds per channel. This turns "lowest sign-change zero found in the declared scan" — the careful, hedged phrase ZS-M48 had to use — into "lowest critical-line zero", certified. Applied to twelve channels it reproduces ZS-M48's independent q \= 4, 7, 13 certifications and supplies the eight of L.

**Established: the eight-channel benchmark.** γ₁(χ₁₆₅) \= 1.269661064979293 ± 3 × 10⁻¹⁶ lies below every V₄ channel, and the certified prime budget of a Weil audit at (0.2, 0\) falls by 27.94 orders of magnitude from ζ, to fewer than ten thousand primes. This is a benchmark pass for one family. **O4 is not closed**, and the paper no longer says it is.

**Established, with its scope written out: two no-gos.** The acceptance window cannot prune, because the prime length spectrum is not uniformly discrete and relatively-compact-window model sets are Delone. No finite-valued roof on a finite Markov partition can prune either. **What v1.1 added to this — that infinite dimension is thereby forced — is withdrawn.** The no-gos constrain the *length group*, not the *dimension*. Deninger's construction is one canonical survivor, not a forced one, and whether a finite-dimensional realisation exists is now **OPEN-M49.1** — the sharpest question this paper leaves, and one it reached by giving up a claim rather than making one.

**Withdrawn: the family law as a theorem.** One-level density governs family averages. It does not govern first-order statistics, joint laws across channels, or the expectation of a minimum. Conjecture M49.FL survives, under two named hypotheses about the point-process limit and the joint law; the ensemble supports it; nothing proves it. And p \= 0.909 does not show that the mechanism is the conductor — it shows only that 165 is not an outlier in a finite, non-independent enumeration. The corpus's own anti-numerology convention says as much, and v1.1 quoted the convention backwards.

**POME-K′: two gates closed, one conditional.** The flow that ZS-M48 asked for exists, is canonical, is prime-blind, has an unbounded roof, and carries the Artin holonomy. Deninger built it, Connes and Consani decorated it, Morishita bridged it. What the corpus supplies is the field. What ZS-M48 supplied — and what no external theorem hands us — is the half-density weight. We now say which is which.

The corpus is used to closing things. This version of the paper closes two gates, opens one better question, and takes three claims back. That is what the arithmetic supports, and it is what the paper says.
